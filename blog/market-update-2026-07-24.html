Arabica futures on ICE Coffee C closed the week at 314.2 ¢/lb, gaining 1.55% over the prior week as speculative long positioning and persistent concerns over the 2025/26 Brazil crop outlook continued to underpin values. For commercial buyers writing forward contracts against FOB Santos Fine Cup lots, the move extends the already elevated cost basis that has defined this procurement cycle. Roasters and importers in the Gulf and Southeast Asia sourcing washed or natural Arabicas should be aware that any further tightening in the BM&F certified stocks data could accelerate nearby differentials, compressing the window for cost-effective spot coverage.

The Conilon and Rio Minas grades shipping from FOB Vitória remained relevant for blenders seeking lower-cost robusta substitution, though Conilon domestic premiums inside Brazil have been firm, reflecting strong internal demand from soluble manufacturers. Buyers with blending flexibility may continue to find relative value in this origin compared to Vietnamese material on a landed-cost equivalent basis, but the spread warrants continuous monitoring given harvest-period volatility.

ICE Sugar No.11 edged up 0.48% to close at 14.76 ¢/lb, a measured move that nonetheless sustains the pressure on refined sugar cost structures across import-dependent markets including the UAE, Sri Lanka, and West Africa. FOB Santos raw sugar offers continue to reflect modest tightness as crushing-season logistics in Center-South Brazil absorb available vessel space. Buyers covering Q3 requirements should factor current freight and insurance assumptions into their landed calculations rather than relying on older freight benchmarks, which have shifted materially in recent months.

Henry Hub natural gas settled at 2.90 $/MMBtu, slipping 0.38% on the week. While US gas pricing has limited direct transmission to physical commodity procurement decisions in most of our core coverage regions, it functions as a useful directional proxy for energy-intensive processing costs and, more relevantly, for ammonia and fertilizer input pricing that will shape next season's crop economics in Brazil and West Africa.

The USD/BRL rate moved to 5.08, firming approximately 0.26% in favor of the real on the week. A stronger real compresses the reais-denominated revenue that Brazilian exporters receive per dollar of FOB value, which historically exerts modest upward pressure on FOB offer levels as origin sellers seek to protect margin. For physical buyers calculating landed cost in dirhams, riyals, or rupees, even incremental BRL appreciation at current futures levels can translate into meaningful shifts in final import cost, and currency trajectory deserves attention alongside futures levels when benchmarking offers.

Looking ahead, market participants will be watching Brazilian weather forecasts for the 2025/26 Arabica flowering window and any revision to UNICA's cumulative crush data for directional cues on sugar. The interplay between BRL dynamics and certified stock drawdowns will likely remain the primary driver of short-term price behavior across both complexes.
