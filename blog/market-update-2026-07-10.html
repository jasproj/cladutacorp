Arabica futures on ICE posted a sharp weekly decline, with the Coffee C contract settling at 337.75 ¢/lb — a drop of 5.38% from the prior week's close. While prices remain historically elevated, the pace of the pullback is meaningful for physical buyers with open pricing windows. FOB Santos Fine Cup differentials have held relatively firm against the futures move, as Brazilian exporters show limited urgency to chase spot business lower at current basis levels. Buyers in the Gulf and Southeast Asia who are working on Q3 and Q4 indent programs should treat this week's softness as a recalibration rather than a directional shift, particularly until harvest logistics out of Minas Gerais and Espírito Santo provide clearer supply signals. FOB Vitória origins — Conilon and Rio Minas grades — continue to see steady inquiry from robusta-blending accounts across Sri Lanka and West Africa, where the cost-in-cup calculus remains sensitive to any meaningful futures correction.

Raw sugar on the No.11 contract eased 1.72% to close at 14.86 ¢/lb, extending what has been a gradual drift lower over recent weeks. For commercial buyers importing into regulated markets across the GCC and ASEAN, the directional signal matters more than the weekly quantum. FOB Santos sugar export programs are pricing into this softer environment, though freight and port congestion variables continue to influence total landed cost in ways that the flat price alone does not capture. Buyers managing tender commitments or replenishment cycles into Q3 should factor current futures levels against their internal price assumptions before seeking firm basis indications from origin.

The Brazilian real strengthened modestly against the US dollar, with USD/BRL moving to 5.11 — down 0.54% on the week. A stronger real, all else equal, compresses the margin incentive for Brazilian exporters to liquidate inventory, which can support FOB differentials even when futures soften. For importers calculating landed cost in USD, the net effect of this week's moves is mixed: the futures decline provides some relief on flat price, but a firmer BRL tempers the pass-through benefit at the origin level. Buyers settling contracts or rolling hedges in the near term should account for continued BRL volatility, particularly as Brazilian fiscal and monetary policy discussions remain active.

Henry Hub natural gas settled at 2.94 $/MMBtu, slipping 2.36% on the week. While a US benchmark is not a direct input for most physical commodity buyers in this region, sustained softness in US gas prices bears monitoring for its indirect influence on global shipping economics, fertilizer input costs for upcoming crop cycles, and energy-intensive processing margins relevant to refiners and roasters in the GCC and Southeast Asia.

Looking into the coming sessions, market attention will focus on whether Arabica futures find technical support at current levels or extend the correction ahead of fresh Brazilian crop estimates. Currency and macro sentiment out of Brazil will remain the key variable shaping the export basis environment across both coffee and sugar.
