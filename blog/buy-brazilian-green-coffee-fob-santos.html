Buy Brazilian Green Coffee Direct: FOB Santos & FOB Vitória

Brazil remains the world's largest coffee producer and exporter, accounting for roughly one-third of global supply in any given crop year. For commercial importers, roasters, and traders operating in the UAE, KSA, West Africa, and Southeast Asia, sourcing green coffee directly from Brazil — rather than through intermediary brokers — offers measurable advantages in price transparency, documentation control, and supply chain reliability. This guide explains how the direct procurement process works, what grades are available, and what to expect when you buy Brazilian green coffee on FOB terms through a US-registered buyer/seller of record.

Why Buy Direct from a US Buyer/Seller of Record?

The distinction between buying from a broker and buying from a principal is commercially significant. A broker facilitates a transaction between two other parties and typically holds no inventory, assumes no title to the goods, and bears limited contractual responsibility. A US buyer/seller of record, by contrast, purchases the coffee outright from the Brazilian exporter, takes title to the cargo, and re-sells it to international buyers under a formal commercial contract. This structure has several practical implications for procurement professionals.

First, you are dealing with a single counterparty for all contractual obligations — shipping instructions, documentation, claims, and payment. There is no ambiguity about who is responsible if a shipment is delayed, a bill of lading is incorrectly issued, or a quality dispute arises. Second, because the US entity operates under US commercial law and maintains established banking relationships, payment structures and trade finance instruments are more straightforward to execute. Third, a US seller of record can often consolidate origin-side logistics, inspection scheduling, and export documentation in ways that a local Brazilian exporter selling directly to a small overseas buyer cannot always manage efficiently.

For buyers in emerging markets or those purchasing at volumes below what major Brazilian trading houses typically prioritize, this structure provides access to quality-graded Brazilian coffees with the procedural discipline of an internationally operating trade company. Green Coffee Grades Available from Brazil

Brazilian green coffee is not a single product. The country produces a wide spectrum of qualities across different growing regions, processing methods, and botanical varieties. Understanding the grade distinctions is essential for matching supply to your specific roasting or blending requirements.

Fine Cup — Alta Mogiana and Specialty-Adjacent Lots

Fine Cup designates the upper tier of Brazilian Arabica, typically sourced from the Alta Mogiana region of São Paulo state and select areas of southern Minas Gerais. These coffees are natural-processed, screen-size sorted (generally 17/18 and above), and cup clean with low defect counts consistent with SCAA classification floors. Fine Cup lots are suitable for single-origin roasting programs, high-volume espresso blends seeking a sweet, low-acid base, and specialty-adjacent commercial applications. These coffees ship through the Port of Santos, Brazil's primary Arabica export terminal.

Good Cup — Commercial Arabica

Good Cup Arabica represents the mainstream of Brazilian commercial green coffee — the grade that underpins blending programs for roasters across Europe, the Middle East, and Asia. Screen sizes are typically 15 and above, with defect counts that meet or exceed New York exchange deliverable standards. Good Cup coffee offers consistent cup character: mild acidity, medium body, nutty and chocolatey base notes, and low bitterness. This grade also moves through FOB Santos and is the most commonly contracted grade for first-time buyers establishing a Brazilian origin program.

Rio Minas — High-Tolerance Processing Grades

Rio Minas coffees originate primarily from the Zona da Mata and lower-altitude areas of Minas Gerais state. They are characterized by higher defect tolerance and a distinct phenolic or medicinal cup note — characteristics that are considered faults in specialty contexts but are actively sought by certain roasting markets, particularly in the Middle East and parts of Africa, where Rio-tainted coffees are preferred for traditional preparation styles. Rio Minas grades ship via the Port of Vitória in Espírito Santo state, which serves as the primary export outlet for lower-grade Arabica and Conilon Robusta.

Conilon Robusta — Brazilian Robusta from Espírito Santo

Brazil's Robusta production is dominated by the Conilon variety grown in Espírito Santo state. Brazilian Conilon has gained international recognition as a high-quality Robusta option, offering cleaner cup profiles than many Vietnamese or Ugandan Robustas while providing the body, crema stability, and caffeine content that espresso blenders and soluble coffee manufacturers require. Conilon exports are routed through FOB Vitória, reflecting the geographic concentration of production in that state.

FOB Santos vs. FOB Vitória: Understanding the Port Routing

Brazil's two primary green coffee export ports serve distinct supply chains, and understanding the difference is relevant to your logistics planning and lead time estimates.

FOB Santos (Port of Santos, São Paulo) is the largest export port in Latin America and handles the majority of Brazil's Fine Cup and Good Cup Arabica shipments. Santos also serves as a major export hub for refined sugar, and container availability at Santos is generally strong given the port's high throughput. Transit times from Santos to major Middle East ports (Jebel Ali, Dammam, Jeddah) typically run 20–28 days depending on the shipping service selected.

FOB Vitória (Port of Vitória, Espírito Santo) handles Rio Minas Arabica grades, Conilon Robusta, and a range of other agricultural commodities including pepper — another product in our direct-sourced commodities portfolio. Vitória is a smaller, more specialized port with reliable container availability for agricultural bulk cargo. Transit times to Gulf and West African ports are broadly comparable to Santos, though specific service frequencies vary by carrier.

Both ports support standard FCL (full container load) 20-foot dry container shipments for green coffee, with containers loaded in jute bags on pallets or floor-loaded depending on buyer specification and fumigation requirements.

Inspection, Documentation, and Quality Assurance

All shipments are subject to SGS inspection at the loading port prior to container sealing. SGS, as an internationally recognized independent inspection and certification body, conducts weight verification, moisture content analysis, and visual/physical assessment against the contracted grade specification. The SGS certificate of quality and weight is issued as part of the standard shipping documentation set and provides buyers with third-party verification that the cargo loaded conforms to the contracted specification.

Standard shipping documentation includes the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, phytosanitary certificate issued by Brazilian agricultural authorities (MAPA), certificate of origin, and the SGS inspection certificate. For buyers in GCC countries, certificates of origin can be legalized or apostilled as required by destination customs authorities.

Minimum Order Quantity and Container Specifications

The standard minimum order quantity for direct FOB procurement is one full container load (1 FCL). A standard 20-foot dry container accommodates approximately 320 jute bags of 60 kilograms each, yielding a net cargo weight of approximately 19.2 metric tons. Jute bag packaging is standard for Brazilian green coffee exports and meets the requirements of most destination country import protocols. Grain-pro or GrainSafe inner liner options are available for buyers with specific moisture-barrier requirements, particularly relevant for shipments destined for humid climates in West Africa or Southeast Asia.

Buyers requiring multiple container lots can discuss shipment consolidation schedules and laycan windows at the time of contracting. We do not publish minimum or maximum volume caps on an ongoing basis — container availability and crop-season logistics are discussed directly during the qualification process.

Payment Terms

Standard payment terms for direct FOB Brazilian green coffee are structured as follows: 30% advance payment upon contract execution and pro-forma invoice acceptance, with the remaining 70% payable against presentation of shipping documents, including the original bill of lading. This structure protects both parties — the advance confirms buyer commitment and covers origin-side procurement costs, while the balance-against-documents mechanism ensures the buyer receives verified shipping documentation before completing payment.

Buyers with established trade finance arrangements through their banking institutions may inquire about letter of credit structures. All payment terms are confirmed in writing within the formal sales contract prior to any procurement action being initiated.
