The Origin Brief
Brazilian Physical Commodities  ·  June 2026

June 2026 finds commodity desks navigating a paradox: a world awash in macroeconomic uncertainty yet structurally short on the soft commodities that matter most. Coffee is correcting off historic highs but the underlying supply story hasn't changed. Sugar is cheap relative to where it was eighteen months ago — and that gap is closing faster than most buyers anticipated. Pepper is quietly re-rating as Vietnamese supply normalizes and Brazilian origin reasserts its quality premium. Three markets, three different setups — one common thread: waiting costs money. Here's what the desk is watching.

01  —  Green Coffee
The correction is real. The supply crisis underneath it is not going away.
ICE 'C' Arabica ~$2.53/lb   ▼ ~18% from Q1 2026 peak, but ▲ ~35% YoY vs. June 2025   |  London Robusta holding firm near $5,000/MT, keeping blending economics tense and Conilon demand elevated.

Driving it. The ICE 'C' pullback from February's record highs has buyers exhaling — but read it carefully. Brazil's 25/26 crop is an on-year cycle, and CONAB's latest estimate offers volume relief, yet late-season flowering stress in Sul de Minas and Alta Mogiana has trimmed fine-cup yields specifically. Colombian output remains below trend. Global certified warehouse stocks are near 25-year lows. The 'correction' is speculative long liquidation, not a fundamental reset. Any disruption to the Brazil harvest window — frost, drought, logistics — snaps this back hard.

For buyers. Spot coverage for Q3 and Q4 is the priority call right now. Roasters who waited out the February spike secured nothing at better prices — they just delayed exposure into a tighter physical market. With Fine Cup differentials holding and Conilon premiums compressing blending flexibility, H2 cover needs to be locked before the July–August harvest pace sets the narrative. Don't let a $0.30 futures correction talk you out of securing physical. The desk is quoting now.

02  —  White Sugar
Thirteen cents doesn't stay thirteen cents when Brazil's crush is running hot and the monsoon question mark is back.
ICE Raw Sugar No. 11 ~13.70¢/lb | London White Sugar No. 5 ~$470/MT   ▼ ~22% from June 2024 highs — but the floor is being tested   |  White premium (No. 11 to No. 5 spread) has widened, favouring direct white sugar procurement over refinery conversion economics.

Driving it. Brazil's Centre-South crush is tracking ahead of pace through May, and that bearish volume story has driven raw sugar to multi-year lows. But the market is beginning to price in risk on the back half. India's monsoon arrival is running uneven — any meaningful shortfall in Maharashtra or Karnataka reignites the deficit conversation that torched prices in 2023–24. Thailand's export capacity remains constrained. The world is not long sugar; it is simply long Brazil for the next 90 days. That window is finite.

For buyers. 13.70¢ is a gift with an expiration date. Refiners and industrial buyers who need Q4 and Q1 2027 coverage are staring at the best FOB Santos value in two years. The spread between current physical and where this market trades if India disappoints is not a small number. Booking ICUMSA 45 now against forward shipment locks in origin-grade quality and pricing before monsoon season writes the next chapter. The desk has prompt and forward positions available.

03  —  Black Pepper
Brazilian black pepper is re-rating. The buyers who moved in Q1 already know why.
Brazil ASTA 570+ Black Pepper ~$4,800–$5,100/MT   ▲ ~15% YoY vs. June 2025 | trending firm through Q2 2026   |  IPC benchmark reflects tightening Brazilian exportable surplus as domestic food sector absorption increases and Vietnamese shipment timelines extend.

Driving it. Vietnam remains the volume story in global pepper, but quality-grade availability out of Ho Chi Minh is uneven and lead times have stretched into 8–10 weeks on ASTA-spec material. Brazil's Espírito Santo crop delivered a solid harvest in volume terms, but domestic food processing demand — driven by Brazil's own protein sector growth — is absorbing a larger share of exportable production than in prior years. Net result: ASTA 570+ FOB Vitória pricing has moved off its 2025 lows and is finding a new floor with genuine buy-side support.

For buyers. European and North American spice buyers who benchmarked 2026 budgets against 2025 lows are facing a gap. The longer the wait, the wider that gap gets. With Vietnamese alternatives carrying extended lead times and inconsistent ASTA certification, Brazilian origin offers both the spec reliability and the shipping efficiency that Q3 procurement windows demand. FOB Vitória positions are moving. The desk recommends covering at minimum 60-day forward requirements before the July–August trading pace tightens offers further.

The Desk View

June 2026 is a buyers' market in price terms only — the structural supply deficits in coffee, the monsoon optionality in sugar, and the quiet tightening in pepper all point in the same direction: current levels are opportunity, not trend. Coffee's futures correction is a window into physical, not a reason to stand aside; sugar at 13.70¢ is a two-year low with a hard catalyst waiting in the Indian subcontinent; and pepper's ASTA premium is re-establishing itself faster than import budgets anticipated. The Claduta desk's directional call for June: buy coverage across all three, weight your largest forward position in sugar while the Brazil-only bear case still holds, and do not exit June without H2 coffee physical locked.
