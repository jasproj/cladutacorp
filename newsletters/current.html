The Origin Brief
Brazilian Physical Commodities  ·  June 2026

A short read on where the three markets we trade sit this month — what's moving them, and what it means if you're covering forward. Two of the three are supply-driven and firm; one has handed buyers a window. Prices below are public exchange and industry benchmarks, for context only.

01  —  Green Coffee
A record new crop lands into the tightest world stocks in 25 years
ICE 'C' Arabica  ~$2.48/lb   ▼ ~31% YoY   |  Robusta firm — inventories near lows

Driving it. Brazil's 2026/27 harvest is being picked now and it's a record — CONAB forecasts 66.7 million bags, the largest on record, with Arabica up ~28% year-on-year and Espírito Santo near 18 million bags. Yet prices haven't collapsed: global stocks sit at a 25-year low and exporters are holding back amid El Niño uncertainty. Robusta/Conilon is the tighter book, with Vietnam in off-season.

For buyers. Prices are well off their 2024–25 highs at the exact moment a record new crop begins loading — a coverage window that may not stay open if exporters keep tightening. Conilon is where to move early.

02  —  Sugar  ICUMSA 45 / 150
A buyer's market — but with a floor under it
London No.5 White  ~$450/MT   ▼ ~13% YoY   |  the refined-sugar benchmark

Driving it. The 2025/26 season swung into surplus — the ISO pegs record global output near 182 million tonnes — and that glut has pushed white sugar to multi-year lows. But the forward picture is turning: weaker ethanol economics are pulling Brazilian mills back toward sugar, analysts have flipped their 2026/27 balance toward a deficit (Green Pool now near −4.3 MMT), and an emerging El Niño threatens rainfall across Brazil, India and Thailand — the three largest producers.

For buyers. This is a buyer's window that may be closing. Prices sit near multi-year lows on this year's surplus, while next season's balance swings to deficit and weather risk builds. Locking forward coverage now — before that re-prices — is the asymmetric move.

Claduta carries: ICUMSA 45 & 150, FOB Santos. 5,000 MT minimum. SGS inspection at load; 30% / 70% wire terms.

03  —  Black Pepper  ASTA 570+
A fourth straight year of shrinking supply
Brazil ASTA 570  ~$6,200/MT   ▲ firm   |  4th year of falling supply

Driving it. Global pepper output has fallen for four straight years and carry-over stocks are at multi-year lows. Vietnam — ~52% of world supply and now facing a smaller 2026 crop — leaves the market thin, and US buyers are already contracting forward for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 delivery. US tariffs on Vietnamese and Brazilian pepper are reshuffling trade flows, while Chinese and Gulf demand stays firm. The result is a tight, firm market.

For buyers. A tight market rewards forward contracting and origin diversification. Leaning entirely on Vietnam concentrates risk; Brazilian ASTA 570 out of Vitória is the diversification play — and the lowest-cost origin for bold grades.

Claduta carries: Black pepper ASTA 570+ / B1 / B2, Halal available. FOB Vitória. 1 FCL (19 MT). SGS at load.

The Desk View

Coffee and pepper are supply-stories — tight stocks, firm prices, a premium on securing volume early. Sugar is the opposite: a surplus has opened a buying window, with an ethanol floor limiting how far it can fall. If you're covering all three, the move this month is to lock pepper and new-crop Conilon while supply is the constraint, and use sugar's softness to take forward coverage before weather re-prices it.
